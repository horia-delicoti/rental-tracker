Add a rental

A rental is a property you record income and expenses against. Every income and expense row is filed under one, and its page shows only its own figures.

The links and note are yours — nothing is ever fetched from them, they are just kept where you look for them. They open in a new tab when you click them.

Deleting a rental deletes the income and expenses filed under it, so it asks you to type the name first. A snapshot is written before every change, which is the way back.

Add a person

These are the people the net profit is divided between — the owners, not the app's users. Nobody needs an account here, and nothing in the ledger is attributed to them.

Share is their percentage of the profit from every rental combined. The shares do not have to total 100 while you are re-dividing them; the panel says what the total is, and what nobody holds.

Tax % is for the fiscal year on screen only — a marginal rate is a fact about a year, not about a person, so each year is set separately.

Removing someone removes their tax percentages for every year with them. A snapshot is written before every change, which is the way back.

Their initials sit on this, and it is their series in the tax chart.

Currencies & rates

Rate = how many units of that currency one unit of the base is worth. GBP 0.79 means $1 = £0.79. The base currency is what every figure in the ledger is already recorded in, so its own rate is always 1.

The ledger is recorded in the base currency and stays in it. Changing a rate converts nothing and restates nothing you have saved — it changes what the header dropdown draws, and what the tax estimator converts a year's share with before stacking it on other UK income. The base currency is locked as soon as there are records stored in it: relabelling them without converting them would turn $1,450 into MX$1,450 in one click.

Rates are yours: Update rates online fills the ones the European Central Bank publishes, and is the only outbound request this app ever makes — on that button, never on a timer and never on boot. Type them by hand instead and nothing leaves the machine. A currency the ECB does not publish keeps whatever you typed.

Restore a snapshot

The server saves a snapshot before every change and keeps the last 30. Restoring replaces the current data; the current state is snapshotted first, so you can undo a restore.
